\subsection{Direct-J Evaluation and Consistency Checks}
Appendix~\ref{app:r2:numerics} specifies the direct-$J$ factorisation and scale grids.
Within each prior-centre model, shared $\gamma$ settings have identical affine means
and unit-variance fields; widths scale by $\sqrt v$.

At all $401$ zero-mean-prior settings, automatic differentiation of the frozen residual map checks
the normal-equation residual independently of the QR/SVD factors:
\begin{align*}
 h_\gamma&=J^\top(r+J\delta_\gamma)+\gamma(\theta+\delta_\gamma),\\
 \eta_{\rm back}&=\frac{\|h_\gamma\|}
 { (\lambda_{\max}+\gamma)\|\delta_\gamma\|
       +\|J^\top r+\gamma\theta\|}.
\end{align*}
All $401$ ratios are below the prescribed $10^{-7}$ tolerance, with maximum
$6.19\times10^{-17}$; absolute residuals, denominators and shift norms are recorded.
The complement shift $\delta_c=-Q_cQ_c^\top\theta$ is checked through $J\delta_c$,
its norm, projection identity and output. All $17$ checkpoints pass, with maximum
scaled defect $5.10\times10^{-16}$. Normwise residuals can miss weak-direction errors;
null membership alone does not establish amplitude or completeness.

The $\theta$-centred checks cover $95$ distinct checkpoint--precision pairs:
baseline, conditional MLE, original and extended evidence selections under both
priors, and five trained-centre roots, merging repeated precisions per checkpoint.
For these checks, use
$h^\theta_\gamma=J^\top(r+J\delta_L)+\gamma\delta_L$ and denominator
$(\lambda_{\max}+\gamma)\|\delta_L\|+\|J^\top r\|$; the maximum ratio is
$9.12\times10^{-17}$. Nonlinear evaluation at $\theta+\delta_L$ leaves $J$, $g$
and covariance fixed. Independent NumPy predictions at nine points per setting
agree within $4.72\times10^{-12}$ absolute. This is a deterministic forward check,
not posterior sampling.

Fields reproduce regional metrics, and spectral sums reproduce regional mean variances.
All $34$ zero-mean-prior evidence selections and one-log-unit sets reproduce from saved
surfaces; all $34$ $\theta$-centred selections reproduce from new factors.

Saved arrays reproduce all $401$ displacement norms and regional variances
(Appendix~\ref{app:r3:centres}). The envelopes enclose every tested stored
intermediate variance field and a prescribed-spectrum example with a null mode
at $49$ interior precisions. Direct fields at the five refined trained-centre roots
also lie within their coverage envelopes. These checks do not certify the original
Jacobian by interval arithmetic.


\subsection{Evidence Sensitivity Protocol}
The six zero-mean-prior sensitivity cases in Appendix~\ref{app:r2:evidence} keep
singular vectors fixed and adjust the observation coherently:
$z'=z+(s'-s)\theta_{\rm modes}$, with $z=V^\top(J\theta-r)$ and
$\theta_{\rm modes}=U^\top Q_1^\top\theta$. For $\gamma=\alpha v$, an assumed
singular-value budget gives
\[
 |\Delta\lambda_i|\le2s_i|\Delta s_i|+|\Delta s_i|^2.
\]
At $s_i=\sqrt\gamma$, the relative envelope is
$2\delta s/\sqrt\gamma+\delta s^2/\gamma$; the $10\times$ case scales its two terms by
$10$ and $100$. Table~\ref{tab:r2-evidence} shows why the extended Allen--Cahn MLP
selections are especially exposed: the $1\times$ quadratic terms alone are
$7.55$, $488.55$ and $228.65$ times $\gamma$. The formed-Gram roundoff scale in Table~\ref{tab:spectrum} is not an error bound for
direct-$J$ QR/SVD.

Every Allen--Cahn Fourier selected pair and the pairs for Burgers--Fourier seeds $0,6$
are unchanged in all six cases. Burgers--Fourier seed $2$ moves its noise and $\gamma$
up one quarter decade in the increase-$10\times$ case. Every Burgers--MLP run changes
the selected pair in at least two cases, retaining its noise boundary; alpha ratios
relative to its extended selection range from $0.562$ to $3.162$ across the cohort.
Allen--Cahn MLP changes in all six cases per run: perturbed noise ratios reach
$177.8$ (seed $0$) and $3162$ (seeds $1,3$), while $\gamma$ ratios reach $1000$ and
$316{,}228$, respectively. Changes in log evidence evaluated at the fixed extended pair
reach about $-1.05\times10^6$. Its extended scales therefore are not presented as robust
scale estimates. Fields at the alternative selected scales use the original Jacobian,
without recomputing a perturbed posterior.

For the fixed-alpha derivative $D_v$ in \eqref{eq:r2-evidence-slope},
the signed terms are grouped by $\lambda_i/\gamma<0.01$, $0.01$--$100$, and $>100$.
Its weak-group energy ratios and counts are reported in Appendix~\ref{app:r2:evidence}.
For a cluster with all $\lambda_i\ll\gamma$, the leading contribution is
$-\tfrac12[m\log v+\sum_i z_i^2/v]$ apart from the constant; calculations retain the full
formula. At fixed $\alpha$ and for this isolated cluster, the stationary variance is
$v_*\approx m^{-1}\sum_i z_i^2$, provided all modes remain weak at $v_*$.
For an exact-null cluster with positive projected energy this expression is exact.
Decreasing $v$ can invalidate $\lambda_i\ll\alpha v$ for positive eigenvalues;
other modes and changed cluster membership affect the full optimum. The expression
therefore explains a local boundary tendency without settling the $v\to0$ limit.
Grouped energies avoid interpreting arbitrary individual coordinates. Allen--Cahn Fourier has substantial
positive transition-group contributions, partly offsetting its negative weak group.
The ratios are descriptive, not goodness-of-fit tests.

The fixed-alpha slope ranges are in Appendix~\ref{app:r2:evidence}; they do not
describe an alpha-reoptimised path or settle the limit $v\to0$.
The largest slope discrepancy against a local central log-noise finite difference is
$4.43\times10^{-6}$ absolute, or $5.51\times10^{-9}$ after division by $1+|D_v|$.
Modal and grouped sums verify the calculation, not evidence-model adequacy.

\subsection{Synthetic Controls}
The small $12\times20$ design compares QR/SVD posterior means, covariances and evidence
with dense calculations, with maximum discrepancies $4.90\times10^{-12}$ and
$3.01\times10^{-12}$ in relative norm and $1.42\times10^{-13}$ in absolute log evidence.
In $1000$ correctly specified prior/noise draws, the affine posterior output mean gives
one-sigma coverage $0.6870$ (draw-level SE $0.00544$) and $95\%$ coverage $0.9534$
(SE $0.00249$). A separate full-column-rank least-squares example with $N=40,P=12$
and generating noise variance $v=0.2$ gives a mean of $0.140411$ (SE $0.001198$)
for the fitted-residual estimator $\|r_{\rm fit}\|^2/N$, consistent with
$(N-P)v/N=0.14$. In a general linear fit the factor is
$(N-\operatorname{rank}(X))/N$, reducing to $(N-P)/N$ only for full column rank.
For a full-row-rank interpolating design it is zero; $P>N$ alone neither establishes
that rank nor supplies a correction for nonlinear PINN fitting.
Fitting can therefore reduce this estimator even under a correct
noise model; this example is not an estimate of PINN discrepancy.

The fixed $100$-draw overparameterised $16$-decade eigenvalue toy has
$100/100$ interior selections agreeing with its prescribed-spectrum reference.
At the $5,25,50,75,95$ percentiles, the selected log-scale ratios
$\log_{10}(\widehat\alpha/\alpha)$ are $(0,0,0,0,0.25)$;
$\log_{10}(\widehat v/v)$ and $\log_{10}(\widehat\gamma/\gamma)$ are
$(-0.25,0,0,0,0.25)$. These quarter-decade-grid medians coincide with the generating
pair without establishing exact scale recovery.

A $21.2$-decade synthetic spectrum with $N=96,P=160$ has $79/96$ prescribed
eigenvalues below $\varepsilon_{64}\lambda_{\max}$. Its generating
$\gamma=10^{-8}$ is interior to the search grid. All $100$ selected pairs are
interior and agree with the prescribed-spectrum reference. The same log-scale
percentiles are $(-0.25,0,0,0,0.25)$ for $\alpha$ and $\gamma$, and
$(0,0,0,0,0)$ for $v$. The maximum selected log-evidence discrepancy is
$9.40\times10^{-8}$. Across the whole tested grid, however, the maximum absolute
discrepancy is $0.01775$ and the maximum scaled discrepancy
$|\ell_{\rm num}-\ell_{\rm ref}|/(1+|\ell_{\rm ref}|)$ is $4.55\times10^{-5}$,
exceeding the prescribed $10^{-7}$ tolerance. Here $\ell_{\rm num}$ and
$\ell_{\rm ref}$ include the Gaussian normalising constant. Near-best discrepancies are
small. Cancellation and the additive constant affect this scaled error; the failed
whole-grid check alone establishes neither an incorrect selector nor model inadequacy.
These controls retain the original tolerance and do not establish PINN evidence stability.
