# Paper statement and theorem map

All 15 principal declarations are in namespace `PaperReliability`. The anchors refer to the accompanying manuscript. Private dependencies remain proved but are not listed as separate contributions.

| Paper anchor | Principal declaration | Verified statement and conditions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Equation (3), conditional covariance representation | `spectral_covariance_representation` | A supplied two-sided orthogonal basis and positive shifted eigenvalues give both inverse identities and the covariance quadratic-form modal sum. The precision is the operator defined by that representation. |
| Equation (7), Theorem 8: fixed-cutoff pointwise sandwich | `two_subspace_contrast_enclosure` | Nonnegative eigenvalues bounded by `Lambda`, positive precision and cutoff, and a positive bulk lower envelope enclose the ratio of pointwise variances. The threshold supremum/infimum consequence is not included. |
| Equation (6), fixed-cutoff regional sandwich | `regional_two_subspace_enclosure` | Exact finite-region means of squared tangent coordinates give the same enclosure for the ratio of mean variances, hence squared RMS uncertainty contrast; positive bulk lower envelope required. |
| Proposition 9: upper witness inequality | `upper_envelope_arbitrary_energy_sharpness` | Prescribed nonnegative sharp/bulk loading energies, positive total bulk energy, and `epsilon < epsilon' <= Lambda` give a four-mode witness with eigenvalues bounded by `Lambda` and approach factor `(gamma+epsilon')/(gamma+epsilon)`. Exact maximum attainment is not asserted in the exported statement. |
| Proposition 9: lower witness inequality | `lower_envelope_arbitrary_energy_sharpness` | The same bounded-spectrum assumptions give a four-mode lower witness with the explicit approach factor; zero total sharp energy is allowed. |
| Equation (13): endpoint variance enclosure | `positive_modal_variance_enclosure` | Positive endpoint precisions, intermediate precision, nonnegative modal coefficients and at least one positive coefficient give the lower and harmonic upper variance envelopes. Zero eigenvalues and zero individual coefficients are allowed. |
| Equation (13): enclosure throughout a retained bracket | `variance_enclosure_on_precision_bracket` | For every supplied `gamma` in `[a,b]` inside the saved endpoint interval, `0 < L_b <= q_gamma <= U_a`. Bracket membership is a hypothesis. |
| Equation (14): bulk-calibrated sharp coverage enclosure | `calibrated_modal_coverage_enclosure` | Exact positive modal fields, fixed errors, finite bulk/sharp samples, a valid supplied higher-quantile rank and nonnegative interval threshold propagate the variance bounds through the empirical quantile and accepted-point counts. No root existence or numerical membership certificate is proved. |
| Appendix C, unnumbered prior-shift decomposition | `kernel_prediction_shift_attribution` | On all exact zero singular modes, the likelihood output shift is zero and the zero-mean prior removes the trained kernel prediction at every positive precision. This does not assert that removing it necessarily worsens prediction. |
| Equation (11): theta-centred predictive displacement | `likelihood_predictive_shift_bound` | Actual modal likelihood shift, positive effective precision and positive noise variance give the covariance-metric Cauchy–Schwarz bound with the separate noise factor retained. |
| Equation (12): theta-centred regional RMS error brackets | `likelihood_regional_error_bounds` | For an actual finite nonempty region, derives the minimum of covariance-metric and modal Minkowski displacement bounds and both error brackets. Signed error is prediction minus reference. The subsequent gap-ratio inference and empirical count are not exported results. |
| Appendix C, unnumbered centre-coverage brackets after (12) | `likelihood_centre_coverage` | The actual modal likelihood shift, its pointwise bound and finite coverage counts give both centre-coverage brackets when the threshold is at least `sqrt(K/v)`. |
| Section 3.3, unnumbered strong-precision limit | `regional_contrast_strong_limit` | The RMS contrast tends at infinity to the square root of the total-energy ratio, with positive bulk total energy. This does not include the first-order expansion (4). |
| Section 3.3, unnumbered weak-precision limit | `regional_contrast_weak_limit` | Through positive precision towards zero, the RMS contrast tends to the square root of the whole-kernel energy ratio, with positive bulk whole-kernel energy. |
| Section 3.3, unnumbered modal counterexample | `endpoint_limits_do_not_bound_contrast` | Nonnegative three-mode data have both endpoint RMS limits equal to one and squared contrast `10304/503 > 20` at precision one. The curve is neither monotone nor antitone on positive precisions. |

The shared `ModalModel` definitions and private steps connect these results. In the Gaussian application, `gamma = alpha * v`; the retained coarse theorems name this modal precision `alpha`. Their role is defined by the formula rather than by that parameter name. The matrix basis orientation and the distinction between the paper's parameter eigenbasis and the SVD's residual-space matrix are detailed in [README.md](README.md).

The higher quantile is the order statistic at a supplied integer rank. To match the paper's NumPy float64 `method="higher"` calls, evaluate `int(np.ceil((n - 1) * np.float64(p)))`, including floating-point rounding, rather than substitute an exact-real ceiling. Theorems include tied observations but do not verify that floating-point rank computation.

The exported witness bounds support sharpness over compatible finite spectra and vectors, not optimisation over a fixed PINN. They do not state `sSup`/`sInf` equalities or exported exact-maximum attainment. Similarly, fixed-cutoff contrast inequalities do not formally include a supremum/infimum over thresholds.

The expansion (4), secondary mean-width/coherence results, Remarks 5–6 and all numerical empirical counts are outside this map. Neither these theorems nor the matrix representation certify numerical eigensolvers, Jacobian arrays, automatic differentiation, rounding error or nonlinear shifted-network evaluation.
